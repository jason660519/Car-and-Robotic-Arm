PAGE 02 Chassis Mode 1

4-Wheel Differential Drive Chassis Overview

The 4-Wheel Differential Drive mode is the most common entry-level robot chassis structure. It controls 4 independent motors to drive each wheel individually, allowing zero-radius pivot turning by spinning left and right wheels at different speeds or in opposite directions.

Note: Since all 4 wheels are fixed in orientation, turning generates tire friction against the ground.

💡
Lock Screw Status: Direction Lock Screw must be FULLY TIGHTENED in Differential mode.

PAGE 03 Chassis Mode 2

Ackermann Steering Chassis Overview

Mimicking real automotive steering geometry, the Ackermann configuration controls front-wheel steering angles via a dedicated servo motor. This setup provides superior stability during high-speed movement and precise cornering control.

It is widely adopted in National University Student Smart Car Competitions and autonomous driving research labs.

💡
Lock Screw Status: Direction Lock Screw must NOT BE INSTALLED in Ackermann mode to allow steering linkage motion.

PAGE 04 Chassis Mode 3

Mecanum Wheel Chassis Overview

Mecanum wheel drive enables full omnidirectional mobility—allowing the robot to move sideways (crab walk), diagonally, and spin in place. It is extensively used in robotics competitions like Robocon and FRC due to its extreme maneuverability.

Note: Requires relatively flat surfaces to maintain roller contact and traction.

💡
Lock Screw Status: Direction Lock Screw must be FULLY TIGHTENED in Mecanum mode.

PAGE 09 - 10 Critical Warnings

Safety Precautions & Critical Assembly Rules

⚠️
1. Acrylic Fragility: Acrylic parts are brittle. Handle with care during assembly to avoid cracking.

2. Locknut Friction Rule: DO NOT overtighten nylon locknuts on revolving arm joints! Joints must swing smoothly. Overtightening increases servo resistance and will burn out / short-circuit the servo motor!

3. Servo Neutral Calibration: Do NOT rotate servo horns manually before installation. Power on the servo to let it return to its neutral 90° position before securing servo arms.

PAGE 11 Hardware Guide

Screw Model Identification & Parts Reference Guide

Most fasteners in this kit are standard GB cross-head (Phillips) screws, primarily categorized into M2 (Black) and M2.5 (Silver).

1. Standard Screw Designation: M [d] * [L]
• d (Thread Diameter): Specifies thread thickness (e.g., M2 = 2.0mm, M2.5 = 2.5mm).
• L (Thread Length): Measured from beneath the screw head to the tip (excluding head height).
• Quick Rule: M2 screws are thinner than M2.5 screws. M2.5*6 screws are shorter than M2.5*8 screws.

2. Comprehensive Parts Reference Matrix:

Mecanum Coupler & Roller Orientation Setup
